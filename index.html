Why Sustainable Transportation Matters Abroad

Transportation is one of the largest contributors to greenhouse gas emissions worldwide — and the choices you make while studying abroad add up. Understanding your options helps you reduce your environmental footprint while often saving money and experiencing the city the way locals do.

Environmental impact of transport emissions

Transportation accounts for roughly 23% of global CO₂ emissions from energy use. A single ride-hail trip can emit 50–70% more carbon per passenger-mile than the equivalent bus or metro journey. Across a full semester, those choices compound significantly.

Public transit, bike share & rail vs. ride-hail

Metro, tram, and regional rail systems move hundreds of passengers per trip with minimal per-person emissions. Bike share adds zero direct emissions for short trips. Ride-hail options like Uber typically carry 1–2 people per combustion vehicle, making per-person emissions 3–5× higher than equivalent rail or bus travel.

How sustainability is graded

A quick comparison rubric for students choosing lower-emission ways to get around.

Grade A

Very low direct emissions or zero-emission shared travel. Applies to: bike share, metro, tram, rail. Electric and human-powered modes at high ridership.

Grade B

Lower-emission shared travel, but not as efficient as top-grade electric or bike options. Applies to: bus, shuttle, mixed public transit. Often diesel-powered but high-occupancy.

Grade C

Shared transport with higher per-person emissions or less consistent occupancy. Applies to: airport transport, road coaches, campus shuttles and other options not clearly in A or B.

Unknown

Insufficient information to assign a grade (e.g. discount cards, eligibility passes, or options without a clearly graded transport mode).
